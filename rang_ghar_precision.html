Rang Ghar Amphitheater (ৰংঘৰ)

Constructed by Swargadeo Pramatta Singha in the royal capital of Rongpur, Rang Ghar ('House of Entertainment') is a two-storied oval pavilion from which Ahom royalty enjoyed elephant fights, buffalo matches, and indigenous wrestling during the Rongali Bihu festivities.

Rang Ghar Amphitheater Heritage

Asia's Oldest Surviving Sports Amphitheater & Royal Ahom Architectural Marvel

Indigenous Organic Mortar (Karoli)

Built with thin fired bricks bound by an organic mortar consisting of sticky Bora rice paste, duck eggs, black lentils (Mati Mah), and fish collagen that has withstood earthquakes for centuries.

Inverted Boat Roof with Crocodile Finials

The distinctive curved roof mimics an inverted royal Ahom longboat, adorned with terracotta crocodile (Makar) finials at both ridge ends reflecting maritime riverine culture.

600-Year Unconquered Dynasty

The Ahom dynasty ruled the Brahmaputra valley for nearly six centuries (1228–1826 CE), famously repelling 17 Mughal invasions through naval warfare on the Brahmaputra River.
